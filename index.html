Abstract

Real-world driving requires context-aware decision-making. Existing end-to-end driving benchmarks primarily assess trajectory quality through safety, compliance, comfort, and driving progress, leaving context-dependent decision-making insufficiently characterized. To fill this gap, we present PlanContext, a benchmark for evaluating in-context driving decision-making. PlanContext constructs paired evaluations by varying high-level driving intentions while keeping the observed scene fixed, enabling a natural and controllable assessment of context-dependent decision-making. We further define six representative driving contexts and jointly evaluate contextual decision correctness, road compliance, and dynamic safety with class-balanced metrics. Experiments on 9,561 scene–condition instances from NAVSIM and 23 public end-to-end driving models show that high conventional driving-quality scores do not consistently correspond to strong route-level in-context driving decision-making. Further analysis shows that model behavior varies with decision timing, road structure, and traffic interaction depending on the decision required by the current context. Our results highlight in-context driving decision-making as an important complementary dimension for evaluating end-to-end driving systems.

How to read the metrics

PDMS / mPDMS: conventional driving quality on the full NAVSIM navtest split / the 5,911 matched physical scenes.

NAS: success in A. CPS: mean success in B1, B2, C. NCS: √(NAS × CPS). LAS / MCS: success in L / LC.

VA: vision/state-to-action; VLA: vision-language-action; WA: world-model-based action. Confidence intervals are from the paper’s scene-level bootstrap analysis.

Main Finding

Driving quality ≠ contextual decision-making

The Spearman rank correlation between mPDMS and NCS is −0.542. High conventional driving quality does not consistently correspond to strong route-level in-context performance.

Six Driving Contexts

When should an intention be followed, deferred, rejected, or an ongoing maneuver preserved?

Sensor observations and vehicle history stay fixed while navigation intentions change. Each paired prediction contains eight future ego poses over a 4-second horizon.

A · FollowAlternative route reachable within 2 s.
B1 · WaitTarget route reachable in 4.5–8 s.
B2 · RejectNo locally feasible target.
C · RejectRoute commitment point already passed.
L · FollowAdjacent target lane is reachable.
LC · PreserveA lateral maneuver is already in progress.

Qualitative Results

Paired predictions under shared observations and changed navigation intentions.

Category A

An actionable alternative

Straight → Left. The alternative route is immediately reachable.

Category B1

Wait for the decision point

Straight → Right. Continue along the current road until the turn becomes actionable.

Category B2

Reject an infeasible intention

Straight → Right. The local road has no feasible right-turn route.

Category C

Preserve the committed route

Left → Straight. The vehicle has passed the route commitment point.
